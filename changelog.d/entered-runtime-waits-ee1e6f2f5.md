### Fixed

- The waits that keep upstream's one-thread order no longer return early for a goroutine the Go runtime has stopped for its own work. `entered.Await`, `entered.Go` and `entered.AwaitUnless` took a goroutine that allocates while a collection marks (it waits for assist credit) or scans its stack, or that a trace or a heap dump stops, for one parked in a wait of the program's, and `entered.AwaitBlocked` also took the world lock of a collection that is starting for one. What a caller could see: `chord/output.AwaitCaller` (`SettleInOrder`, `SettleAndAwait`) went on to settle the next `details()` waiter of a commit before the caller of the one before had run to its next await, so the continuations ran out of Pi's order; `durable/codemode` `spawnEntered` returned before the tool call it started was running, so the call that followed it, or the end of the script that aborts it, could come first; and the chord host's `goEntered` closed its channel before the handler had run to its first wait. It needed a collection or a trace at that instant, so it was rare.
