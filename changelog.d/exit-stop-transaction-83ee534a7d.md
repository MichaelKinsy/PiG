### Fixed

- The exit's terminal restore is one uninterrupted sequence of writes now: a title an extension sets, an OSC 52 copy and the terminal progress keepalive, which three other goroutines wrote at any moment, no longer land inside the stop's bytes (between the cursor show and the bracketed paste off, between the alternate screen's leave and the transcript print) and, when SIGTERM or SIGHUP ended the session, no longer follow them. Pi's `stop()` is one synchronous call on a single thread, so nothing of its own event loop writes inside it and, after a signal, `process.exit(0)` follows it at once. A recording of the exit, or a terminal that handles the bytes in order, saw an `OSC 0`, an `OSC 52` or an `OSC 9;4` between PiG's reset sequences or after the last of them; the screen a user sees is the same. A write requested before the stop begins is still written, before it (a copy requested just before quit reaches the clipboard); one requested while it runs waits for it and, for a quit by keys, follows it as in Pi (the title Pi's dispose sets); one requested after a signal's stop is dropped, as the exit drops it in Pi. An external editor's or a suspend's stop holds the terminal the same way and gives it back when the TUI starts again.
