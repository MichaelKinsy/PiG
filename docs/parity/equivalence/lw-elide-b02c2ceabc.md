# lw-elide equivalence (slice S28, base b02c2ceabc)

The executor's reaction order, every microtask and I/O-completion boundary, every idle point and every byte that depends on them are unchanged. What changes is which stack runs a reaction and how often a coroutine body switches to the goroutine that drives it.

## What changed

| Change | Why the order is the same | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| A pausing coroutine settles on its own stack (`continuationTurn.settle`, `ai/continuation.go`) | When a body suspends or detaches, its driver would run a drain: poll the watchers, pop the next reaction in Node's order (`popLocked`), run it, and so on until execution is owned or nothing is ready. `settle` runs exactly that loop on the pausing body's stack, with the same watcher polls, tick phases and trace hooks, for as long as the next reaction is one that resumes no other body: the body's own grant or acquire, a callback, a promise reaction, an I/O completion, a grant that only marks a turn whose body is not waiting, or one more trip of another turn's owed await (below). At the first other reaction it stops with the queue exactly as the driver's drain would find it, and the body pauses as before. A coroutine that a reaction grants meanwhile waits for the driver (`settling`: no driver goroutine is woken), so it runs where the drain would have run it. | `TestDeferredAwaitsKeepTheSchedule` (new: 300 random streams, settle on and off, identical schedules), `TestSettleResumesWithoutASwitch` (new), `TestExecutorScheduleMatchesBaton` |
| Drain frames (`drains`, `frameless`, `drainLocked`) | The idle hook marks the end of a drain, so the drain frames must match too. A drain that a settled body ends in place (a detach whose release left nothing to run) makes the drain suspended under it return without touching the executor (`drains` moved). A body resumed in place by the drain its driver would have started carries that drain (`frameless`): the driver goes on with it when the body next pauses or returns (`advance`), as that drain would have. | idle points are part of the differential schedule; `TestCodexSSEObservationMatchesNodeOracle` waits on them |
| Owed awaits (`deferResolved`, `awaitResolvedThen`, `grant`) | `k` awaits of an already resolved promise with no code between them are made as one park: the grant re-queues itself behind every queued reaction `k-1` times (or goes on at once when nothing is queued, as `awaitResolved` does), each trip with the trace's microtask, the watcher poll and the end of a tick phase it would have had. A body defers an await only while it holds execution and only when its code up to its next await reads and writes nothing another turn or a host can observe; every await primitive (`awaitResolved`, `awaitContinuation`, `awaitSettled`, `jsAwait`, `detach`), a body read or close (`nativeBodyIterator`), a provider-event observer and the end of the body make the owed ones first, and `suspend` refuses to park owing any. A check the body runs between its owed awaits and the next one (`awaitResolvedUnless`: is a provider-event observer wanted) runs at the body's place, in the grant, so it reads the state another turn may have changed in between. | `TestDeferredAwaitsKeepTheSchedule`: owed and real awaits, with and without settle, interleaved with consumer yields, nextTick waits, an extension handler that awaits in a callback, a timer, an abort mid-record, a provider-event subscription toggled by the consumer and I/O completions; identical schedules in all four modes |

Adoption of owed awaits by the openai-completions decoder is a separate change in `ai/openai.go` (slice lw-sse): its six generator awaits per record read and write only the decoder's own state.

## `settle` and the drain flag (lost-wakeup fix)

`settle` saves `draining` and `drainer` at entry and puts them back when the body goes on to pause, because the drain that resumed the body in place is suspended under it. A body a serving goroutine claimed (`needsDriver`) has no such drain under it: the drain that granted it runs on another goroutine and ends as soon as it notices that a turn holds execution. When that end landed inside `settle`'s unlocked reaction window, the put-back set `draining` again with no loop on any stack, and `serve` and `startDrainLocked` then refused to start one, so the queued reactions never ran (seen as a hang of `TestGoogleVertexProviderStreamMatchesPi` under `-race`). A holding body now settles only when `drainer` is its own driver (the drain it carries, in place or frameless); with any other live drain it pauses at once, as it did before `settle`, so the queue goes to that drain or to the driver. The reactions run, and their order, are the ones the pause would have handed over; this only withdraws the stack-local shortcut in a race where the drain's owner is another goroutine. The same guard keeps a body granted from another body's `settle` (a grant that flipped to `needsDriver` between `settlesInPlaceLocked` and `grant`) from running a second reaction loop. Proof: `TestContinuationSettleDoesNotResurrectEndedDrain` forces the interleaving with `executorTrace.afterReaction`.

## Known difference (not observable by a Pi program)

Reactions a settled body runs execute on that body's coroutine instead of on its driver goroutine. A drain already ran on whichever goroutine posted or served; no reaction depends on its goroutine.
