# The two Linux test-conformance jobs run these twenty shards, ten each, one after another, each its own go test process under go test's 10-minute package
# timeout. Every shard process shares the extension cells (testenv.SharedCellCacheDir, built in TestMain), so a shard's wall is its tests' own time plus about
# 20 s of process overhead. Cells built once per process instead of once per job were ~75 s of each of the 28 shards of an earlier layout, which made each job
# 12 minutes longer than the 12-shard layout and made a test's wall depend on whether it was the first in its shard to need a fixture.
# The weights are generated, not tuned by hand, and can be checked: automation/ci/test-shard-timings/extension-conformance.json holds the timings they come
# from (the go test -json of a warm run of every shard of the previous 20-shard layout, four containers of 4 CPUs at once: for each test its shard, elapsed
# seconds, the sum of its subtests' and whether it is t.Parallel; and each shard's wall). automation/ci/shard-pack.py fits a model of a shard's wall to them,
# wall = 19.7 + 1.015*serial + 0.292*parallel test time, with an error on the 20 fitted shards of 5% on average and 13% at worst (the same fit on the 28-shard
# run of the first layout: 8% and 30%, and a cold run varies up to 28% on a shard that a contended host slowed). It pins the tests of 5 s or more by
# longest-first packing on top of the hash-placed rest and picks the fewest even shard count whose modelled worst shard stays at or under half of the 600 s
# limit on a runner 1.4x slower than the host that took the timings, allowing 20% model error (179 s): 18 shards would model at 190 to 192 s, which that
# error takes to 230 s and 322 s on the slower runner, so the count is 20 (130 pins, model 173 to 175 s). 30 s are reserved on the first shard of each job for
# the one-time cell build of a cold job, which TestMain does once in the first process. Regenerate when the suite changes: run every shard warm with
# go test -json into a directory, then
#   automation/ci/shard-pack.py --package ./test/extension-conformance --emit-timings automation/ci/test-shard-timings/extension-conformance.json <dir>...
#   automation/ci/shard-pack.py --package ./test/extension-conformance --cold 30 --cold-shards 1 --shards auto --error 1.2 --replace-shards 20 --write <this file> automation/ci/test-shard-timings/extension-conformance.json
# (shard-pack.py states its model, errors and loads; automation/ci/shard_pack_test.py tests it.)
# Measured in containers of 4 CPUs, fresh checkouts and cold caches, make test-conformance-1 and -2 (four jobs side by side for the twelve shards and the previous
# weights, two for these): the twelve shards took 1,987 and 2,019 s a job (shards 268 to 355 s); these twenty
# took 1,758 and 1,743 s with the previous weights (shards 92 to 201 s) and 2,000 and 2,063 s with these (shards 124 to 239 s) on a host that other work had made slower, where the prerequisite builds alone took 349 s against 180 s; the worst shard, 40% of the limit and 56% on a 1.4x slower runner, ran 36% over its model, as one shard did in the review's cold runs.
